and --- in Section~\ref{sec:valscope} --- how much of each value space that

step-by-step procedure behind every block-level bench.

ModelSim; a second flow runs the same system testbench under Verilator to obtain
assertions and functional coverage, neither of which the free ModelSim edition

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

Four properties of the method decide how much a passing run is worth.

\paragraph{Counts are exact, not approximate.} The direct-mode synchronous
handler counts every entry and must reach exactly 17; a register accumulates one
bit per cause and must equal \texttt{0x1FF}; \reg{mhpmcounter6} independently
counts every trap entry of any kind and must equal exactly 26 (17 direct, 1
vectored, 8 interrupts). A missing, doubled or spurious trap cannot cancel
against another.

\paragraph{Negative properties get a witness, and a paired positive.} ``No
transaction was issued'' and ``the interrupt was never taken'' cannot be observed
directly, so the benches count what did happen --- AXI handshakes, acknowledge
pulses, handler entries --- and require the count to be zero. Every such check is
then followed by the step that removes the suppressing condition and requires the
thing to happen, which is what separates masking from a dead path.

\paragraph{A read-only check is only as good as its precondition.} Writing zero
to a register that already reads zero and then observing zero proves nothing.
Every read-only register is driven to a known non-zero value by real hardware
activity before the write is attempted.

\paragraph{Configurations are swept, and coverage is gated.} The suite runs under
four bus-timing configurations, so a result that depends on latency is exposed
rather than hidden, and each run echoes its parameters read back from the
elaborated design --- a silently ignored override otherwise looks exactly like a
pass. Required coverage bins must be hit or the run fails.

A note on the first row, because it is the clearest argument for the assertion
layer existing at all: the procedural monitor could not have found it. Monitors
arm after reset, and the violation occurs during reset.

\paragraph{The two benches added in this revision found no RTL defect.} That is
worth stating plainly rather than leaving the table to imply it. \file{tb\_bp}
and \file{tb\_alu} were written to close documented behaviour that nothing else
exercised --- the return-address-stack boundaries, the \sig{RAS\_DEPTH}~=~0
tie-off, the predictor's reset values, and the ALU's decode and operand
boundaries --- and the RTL was correct in every one of those cases on the first
run. Both benches were instead validated by mutation, described in
Sections~\ref{sec:vp-bp} and \ref{sec:vp-alu}: the RTL was deliberately broken
in one place each and the bench was required to fail. A bench that has never
failed is not yet evidence of anything.
